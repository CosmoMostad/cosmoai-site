# Hearts: pro-level decision making

This is the playbook the coach encodes. Read it once, then let the overlay remind you at the table.

## Passing

Rank every card by how much it hurts you if you keep it. Pass the three worst. In order:

1. **Unprotected Q♠.** The Queen with fewer than three lower spades is a 13-point liability: two spade leads and you are forced to play it under nothing. With three or more low spades, keep it. You decide when it goes, usually onto a trick someone else is already winning.
2. **Bare A♠ / K♠ when you do not hold the Queen.** They win a spade trick and the Queen lands on them. With three or more low spades under them they are survivable.
3. **High hearts (A K Q J).** They win heart tricks. Long hearts (five or more) soften this because you have lows to hide behind.
4. **Voids.** A singleton or doubleton in clubs or diamonds is worth passing even if the cards are low. A void lets you throw the Queen or a high heart on that suit every time it is led.
5. **A♦ / K♦, then A♣ / K♣.** Diamonds get led late, when hearts are being dumped. High clubs can often be shed on the first trick, which can never carry points, so they are less urgent. If you hold the 2♣ you lead trick one and lose that free dump.

Never pass low spades (2 to 10). They are the safest cards in the game: they let you follow spade leads without ever winning the Queen, and they let you lead spades to smoke the Queen out of another hand.

Never pass low hearts (2 to 5) or low clubs and diamonds unless the void is worth it.

**Direction matters.** The player you pass to right plays before you every trick, so if you give them the Queen you see it come out before you commit. The player on your left plays after you and can drop it on you. Pass the Queen right when you can, left only when it is genuinely unprotected.

**Moon check.** Before passing, ask whether the hand can take every point: three or more top hearts, A♠ or K♠ with the Queen, aces and kings in the side suits, and few low losers. If so, pass your lowest losers instead of your dangerous cards and keep every winner. The coach shows a moon score and the alternative pass.

## Playing

### The first trick
No points can fall. Whoever has the 2♣ leads it. If you follow, unload your highest club for free. If you are void in clubs, throw the A♠ or K♠ (the Queen and hearts are not allowed).

### Leading
- **Hunt the Queen.** While the Queen is out and you hold only spades below it, lead your low spades. It costs nothing and every spade lead brings the Queen holder closer to being forced.
- **Never lead spades while you hold the Queen** unless you have five or more. Each lead burns cover.
- **Never lead A♠ or K♠ with the Queen out.**
- **Lead the lowest card in play.** If you hold the lowest remaining card of a suit it cannot win. If every higher card is already gone, it will.
- **Avoid suits an opponent is void in.** They will dump the Queen or hearts on you. The coach tracks voids from every failure to follow suit.
- **Lead singletons early** to open voids while there is still time to use them.
- **Cash winners early when it is safe.** Once the Queen is gone and everyone still follows a suit, an ace wins a clean trick now and becomes a liability later.
- **Stop the moon.** If one player has taken every point so far, take one heart trick, even at a cost of three or four points. It saves you 26.

### Following suit
- **Duck as high as you can.** Play the highest card that stays under the current winner. It preserves your lowest cards for when you really need them.
- **Last to play on a clean trick: take it with your highest card.** No points can fall, you shed a high card, and you get to lead. The exception is when winning leaves you on lead with no safe exit.
- **Forced to win, not last: play the lowest card that wins** so a later player can still overtake.
- **Forced to win, last: play your highest.** You are winning regardless.
- **Holding the Queen on a spade lead:** if an A♠ or K♠ is already on the table, drop the Queen. Otherwise duck under with a lower spade and keep her hidden.

### Discarding (void in the led suit)
1. The Q♠, always, unless you are shooting.
2. A♠ or K♠ while the Queen is out.
3. Your highest heart.
4. A high card from your most exposed suit, or a singleton to make another void.

Never feed hearts to a player who is shooting the moon. Give them a harmless card and make sure someone else takes a heart trick.

### Counting
Keep three numbers in your head: how many hearts are out, where the Queen is, and which suits each opponent has shown out of. The coach displays all three every trick.
